How I got paid to write blog posts

Hello Reader—

Forbes paid me to write a column for two years.

I wrote blog post after blog post about productivity and leadership.

Each post averaged 600–800 words.

I spent two hours researching, writing, editing, and publishing each blog post.

To keep my commission…

They tasked me with writing at least seven posts a month.

And for all my column ideas turned blog posts?

I got paid $500 a month.

That’s about $7 an hour.

Or 10 cents a word.

(Forbes sometimes paid a small bonus if a post went viral)

After two years, a Forbes editor rang me.

He said they were killing my column.

At first, I was annoyed.

Hundreds of hours of work... GONE!

But then, I was relieved.

$7 an hour was hardly quit my day job money, which paid quite a bit more.

I got social proof by writing for Forbes…

But I didn’t need to keep banging my head against the wall.

These days, I still write blog posts for my site.

I sometimes write articles on Medium like this one and get paid for that, too.

But I don’t write these posts for money.

(At least, not directly…)

I write to articulate ideas in public… and build relationships with you.

Look:

Earning a living writing blog posts is a grind.

And it’s harder than ever now, thanks to AI.

That said, I don’t want to leave you hanging.

Dozens of websites will pay you to create blog posts today.

Here’s a mega list.

I also recommend using a service like Writers Work.

It contains lots of paying writing gigs for wannabe bloggers and freelancers.

But my honest take?

Use these resources IF you need a few bucks FAST.

Then, reframe blogging to connect with readers and practice in public.

To get paid what you’re worth, don’t just write blog posts for someone else:

Do your own thing.

Creating an offer for your audience.

A course.

Coaching.

A paid newsletter.

They’re all more lucrative than any column or part-time blogging gig.
