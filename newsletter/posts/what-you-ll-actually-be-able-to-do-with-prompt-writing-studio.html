What you'll actually be able to do with Prompt Writing Studio

Feature lists are boring. Let me tell you what changes.

Before PWS, a typical AI session for most writers looks like this:

Open ChatGPT. Type "write me an intro about X." Paste the output into your draft. Rewrite 70% of it because it sounds like everyone else's writing. Feel slightly worse about AI than you did an hour ago.

After working through the course:

You stop writing one-shot prompts. You build small libraries of prompts you reuse for your actual work — your newsletter, your client emails, your book research, your blog posts.

Specifically, you'll be able to:

  • Write a prompt that forces AI to sound like you, not a generic LinkedIn influencer
  • Assign a role and output spec so you stop getting five-paragraph essays when you asked for three bullet points
  • Turn a messy brain-dump into a structured outline in one pass
  • Take a 60-minute podcast transcript and extract three pieces of content from it
  • Build a "coach" prompt that interviews you before writing, instead of guessing
  • Convert recurring tasks (weekly reviews, meeting notes, research briefs) into repeatable workflows
  • De-slop AI prose so it reads like a human wrote it

What you won't get:

A course on how to become an AI influencer. A thousand generic ChatGPT "hacks" you've already seen on LinkedIn. Motivational filler. Anything about how AI is going to change the world.

Just the prompts, frameworks, and workflows I actually use in my own business.

Who it's for:

Writers, coaches, business owners, and creators who are using AI now and getting mediocre outputs. If you haven't opened ChatGPT yet, this isn't the right course — start with the free quiz on the site first.

Who it's not for:

Engineers building AI apps. People who want to debate alignment. Anyone expecting fine-tuning or API tutorials.
