# EVAL.md — the sceptical-subscriber read (2026-07-01, overnight)

Persona: signed up in 2021 for writing help via a "101 writing prompts" style
lead magnet. Hasn't clicked anything in months. Opens the launch email because
the subject mentions an apology.

## The objections, taken seriously

**"I signed up for writing advice. Who is this builder guy?"**
Partially defused by history, not by copy: the newsletter has been AI/builder
content since at least March 2026, at stable 32-36% open rates. The people this
pivot was going to lose are inside the 241 unsubscribes/quarter already. This
subscriber has watched the bridge get built for months; the product is the
first time they're asked to pay to cross it. And what they're asked to buy is
still, at its heart, a *content* system: ideas, drafts, newsletters. The
builder tech is the delivery mechanism, not the subject. Verdict: the bridge
holds, but only because the newsletter walked it first. This product would
have read as abandonment in 2024. In mid-2026 it reads as consistent.

**"You sold me a 'closing forever' course eight weeks ago and now you're
selling again."**
This is the sharpest objection and it's legitimate. Three things keep it from
landing as a cash-grab:
1. The launch email leads with the apology for a broken promise, not with an
   offer. The product exists because 305 people clicked and got nothing.
   Fixing that is the opposite of manufacturing demand.
2. Zero scarcity mechanics. The page and email explicitly commit: no
   countdown, no closing date, same price next month. That is a direct,
   visible break from the May playbook.
3. The warm-up email gives away the flagship prompt in full, no signup, no
   link to the product. A cash-grab doesn't hand out its core asset first.
Residual risk: a subscriber who buys the $39 product and later learns PWS
went from "$197, closing forever" to closed may wonder what "forever" means
in this shop. Nothing tonight can fix that; only consistent behaviour can.

**"$39 for prompts I could write myself?"**
The warm-up email lets them test exactly that for free. What the $39 buys is
disclosed precisely: five more prompts, six working scripts, the plumbing
(Kit scheduling, transcript fetching, file management), templates, samples,
a beginner-proof guide, and 30-day refunds. No income claims, no "this will
transform your business". The page even names who should NOT buy. Honest.

**"Python? I'm a writer, not a programmer."**
Manual mode is the answer and it's structural, not a footnote: every script
ships as a paste-into-Claude prompt, it's on the page in its own highlighted
card, and the requirements section says "read this before buying". The
warm-up email demonstrates manual mode working before any money is asked.

**"Will this be abandoned like the courses were?"**
Fair, given products.html shows two ENROLLMENT CLOSED stubs. Mitigation: this
is a one-time download, complete on delivery, with no cohort, drip, or
community to maintain. The failure mode of Bryan's attention (real, per his
own worklog) can't strand a buyer the way it strands a course.

## Verdict

**Genuine evolution, not a cash-grab — with one condition.** The product is
real work, priced at the honest end of his historical band, sold with the
scarcity dial at zero, to an audience whose click data explicitly asked for
it. The apology framing only works because it is factually true, and the
warm-up email gives before it asks.

The condition: the "no countdown, same price next month" promise and the
30-day refund line are now public integrity commitments. They are what
separates this launch from the May one. If a future email walks them back,
this launch retroactively becomes the cash-grab it currently isn't.
