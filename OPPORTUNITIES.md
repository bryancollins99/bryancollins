## Winner: #1, The Builder's Pipeline — and it isn't close

1. **It's the only candidate with direct, price-specific demand evidence.** 305 people
   (7.8% of openers) clicked "Get The Builder's Pipeline ($39, one-time)" in March. Every
   other candidate's revenue column is inference; this one is measurement.
2. **It honours a broken promise.** The product was announced, priced, and linked — and the
   link died. Shipping it converts the mission's biggest integrity risk (another pitch
   8 weeks after "closing forever") into an integrity *story*: "I owe you this."
3. **The code exists.** 2,626 lines across exactly the 6 scripts the email promised, secrets
   already env-based. Tonight's work is genericising, documenting, and packaging — the
   highest-certainty build on the board.
4. **It is the bridge.** A writing-era subscriber buys a *content* system (ideas, drafts,
   newsletter scheduling — their world); the delivery mechanism (Python + Claude API) *is*
   the builder direction. Old audience served, new direction demonstrated, nobody abandoned.
5. **Zero-sales delivery.** One-time download; Kit Commerce already has Stripe wired and
   tags buyers automatically.

**Mitigation for the main weakness** (technical bar for a writing audience): every script
ships with a **manual-mode prompt** — the same prompt the script runs, formatted to paste
straight into Claude. A buyer who never installs Python still gets the full system's value.
This is disclosed on the landing page, not hidden.

**Price: $39** — as publicly promised in March (repricing a promised product upward would
poison the integrity story; downward would punish the 305 who tried to buy). Sits inside
the $20–47 band that actually sells to this list.
